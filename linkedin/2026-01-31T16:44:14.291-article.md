The HOW is oftentimes more important than the WHAT. When you picture a majestic statue of a lion, you likely do not envision the image below as an acceptable result. Yet, this reconstruction portrays the state of the art in ancient Greece, it is neither a caricature nor an experiment gone wrong.


Today, nobody in their right mind would place such a statue in front of a building to convey strength and respect. However, producing it still requires significant effort that the artist would expect to be paid for. There might even be additional costs, such as moving and installing the piece, because one assumes that if an artist decides to install a work, it meets a certain aesthetic standard. The moving company, of course, would expect payment regardless of the statue's artistic merit.


Custom technical systems face similar challenges. Everyone involved relies on the trust that all parties will do their best and strive for a result that matches the industry's state of the art. While you can specify many requirements for high motivation, best intentions, pleasing aesthetics, robustness, accurate task estimates, and timely communication you will be hardly able to enforce them.
