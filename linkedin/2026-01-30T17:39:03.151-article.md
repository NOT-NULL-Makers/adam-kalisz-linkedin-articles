Test Driven Development is mostly a net inefficiency. In many cases, it's just gold-plating to charge more with diminishing returns. Specification or Spec Driven Development starts to make a lot more sense these days and many of us practice it without knowing.


When practitioners hear the word #specification it may evoke legalese or some high-stakes or academic fields. With the proliferation of LLM-assisted development specifying what we want to do instead of how we want to do it becomes mainstream.


And of course, we want to be able to enforce the rules. Perhaps even generate examples, which follow or break these to test all the happy and sad paths. Now look into your contractor time sheets and count all the unit tests you have paid and will pay for even when they are LLM generated.


Can you somehow generate more of those that test something qualitatively different? How about the inverse case? What do you have to do when you change the code that the unit tests test? Can you use the unit tests to validate data at runtime, e.g. when you receive something from an untrusted part of the system such as an app or website client?
