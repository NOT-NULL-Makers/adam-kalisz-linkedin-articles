Who does benefit from coordinated vulnerability or ”responsible” disclosure?


Mostly the company or the project that put the security hole, the defect into the software in the first place. Some researchers do get a bug bounty but most just spend their time following processes suitable for the company or the project with no reward maybe besides a short mention somewhere. On the other hand, they are sometimes targets of lawsuits or accusations.


From my point of view to try to disclose a security bug in private using the processes is a nice thing to do but any disclosure is overwhelmingly a good thing. If it's out there you can theoretically know about it and fix or mitigate against it. Remember, any security hole is there, because the company or the project wrote the software that way. It's not like breaking into a vault where somebody drills a hole, it does not work like that with software.


A software with no known security holes is not automatically secure. It can just mean, nobody looked or nobody disclosed the findings or the holes are sufficiently hard to find.  It can also mean the software has so many holes that the person doing some kind of validation or an audit came to the conclusion that the software is unfit for purpose and did not bother to disclose the fact.


So what about if a security hole can endanger users of the software?


You don't know who was the first person to find the hole before the disclosure you just hope it was not misused. And also most people do not patch immediately so there is always time between disclosure and running the software with the fix, sometimes months or years.


And if it's critical software then there should have been other mitigations in place or you just don't have a proper threat model, sorry.


If you are the user, maybe don't use software or services that have a history of security incidents. You also don't need to put your data everywhere. In the EU, you have the right to know what data is where, to correct it if there are mistakes or to delete most of it thanks to GDPR, so use your rights.
