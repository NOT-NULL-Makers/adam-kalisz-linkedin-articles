Build diverse sales people teams and make sure you send two instead of one to as many negotiations as possible. This will enable your side to always have at least one person listening for what your customer or partner tries to communicate. Here is how this will save you time and money.


👉 Don't write offers that don't land.


Why would you write an offer, if you don't understand your potential customer or partner. Listen carefully, explore the options.

• Do they want the standard thing or do they need a very custom deal?

• How will your premium pricing be understood by them in a relative vacuum?

• How do you make it a win-win and prevent your loss?


👉 Avoid bad reputation because of unfortunate perception.


The customer may communicate badly. Make sure you really know what they want, it can be different from what you imagine based on what they've said so far. Confirm your assumptions from multiple angles. Don't draft contracts before that.


👉 Make sure your people know who they are dealing with and how to handle situations when they don't know.


If you invite a customer to your premises make sure they have a smooth experience. That beverages are offered and if you have e.g. shared paid parking that their ticket is taken care of, especially when you are speaking about a deal that is 10,000x bigger in value then the expenses. It's not about the money, it's about chores and the signal this sends.
