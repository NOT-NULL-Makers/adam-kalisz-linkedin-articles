A representation of the primary and secondary DNS.


You get two of something, they should be equal but somehow they aren't. Excuse this somewhat graphic depiction but it's the same for public resolvers like Google Public DNS, Cloudflare DNS, Quad9 DNS, OpenDNS and the same within company domains with the primary directory controller usually experiencing disproportionately more traffic.


For some reason, people just tend to put all eights, all ones, all nines first and because name resolution usually doesn't do a round robin but works in a failover fashion, unless you configure your hosts to use the secondary first, you will contribute to the imbalance.


For anycast networks it shouldn't matter much as the load is probably spread over the same servers in the nearest point of presence. It's just the routing in BGP that is different for the previously named providers. For DNS4EU it's all in the same subnet.
