Do you also feel the pain of managing multiple accounts into a single system, for a platform? Every time you want to add an appointment into a calendar from an invite it opens the wrong account and switching to the right one takes ages?


🪣 The fundamental dilemma is #compartmentalization or the division of something into sections or categories according to the dictionary. Compartmentalization allows keeping things manageable inside a topic but risks introducing the need for management of the compartments or buckets, e.g. when you need to cross into other topics and switch contexts.


🗃️ The other friend of compartmentalization is #segmentation, the process of dividing a larger group into smaller groups based on shared characteristics.


👉 They are both the essence of the #divide and conquer strategy. That however assumes you can tackle one bucket at a time and stop caring about the ones you already touched. In the digital age, we are burdened by the need to keep a lot of stuff around for what feels like eternity and risking a spaghetti of interconnected components and systems.


🧹 On the example of household chores, you can use multiple buckets for cleaning depending on if you want to mop or clean other surfaces by hand. Now you have to store these buckets somewhere in your small apartment when you are not cleaning or you have to be more mindful of all the buckets full of water when cleaning to not spill water on you nice wooden floor.

You can buy milk in one liter or half-gallon containers but then you have to manage the empty containers. If you are at a conference, you only have these little milks of less than 10 g each, so if you are like me you need 4 to have a coffee or tea the way you like. All this fiddling and waste!


⚙️ In IT terms, you can segment disk space into blocks, now you need a filesystem. You can have multiple accounts, now you have to switch between them, so you introduce profiles or specific environments that you have to manage. You can segment a network for security, now you need to do routing, manage Access Control Lists or firewall rules. You can use an array instead of a hash map or a linked list, now you have to right size everything.


🤔 When compartmentalizing, ask yourself:

• Is there a need for a strong boundary?

• Can we avoid this split while fulfilling our goals?

• Is the problem space self-contained in the container or module?

• Can we view the container as a black box?

• What is the cost of managing these compartments?

• Can we reasonably change the size of the compartment?

• How hard will it be to undo the compartmentalization?
