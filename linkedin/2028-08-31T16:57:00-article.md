Open your cold outreach with “I'll keep this quick” and land in the trash.


👉 Because you are wasting message real estate,

👉 are admitting to being a disturbance,

👉 set low expectations for the value.


And of course the message often is neither short nor quick to read and if you can't even avoid the anti-pattern in the first sentence, the rest never made me reconsider and not hit the trash or “not interested” button.


So how do you open better?


Show genuine interest and make it smooth for the other party to arrive to the conclusion that you have something of value that they want.


“But I don't have time for that” you might say. Then don't do cold outreach or lead generation and expect high-value results. You can sell some cheap junk with this approach but you will be ignored by most serious contacts.


If you want to save some time, you can let AI prepare a dossier and a summary for each of those contacts. But that is a skill in itself and is not 100% so you may again burn those contacts at an unsustainable rate if you want to stay in the industry for some time.


What does work quite well is to congratulate on a real achievement and invite people to some party, ideally telling them who else from their network is also joining. People love free parties, but they have to be good (that doesn't mean expensive necessarily) to stand out.


If the person is busy, you may ask a question targeting a “No” answer, perhaps followed by an easy “Yes”, confirmation.


“Would you see it as spam if I sent you this experience report on that topic? Would e-mail ... be suitable for that?”


And of course, don't follow up with “did you have time to look at”, that's pushy. Just share one more thing that is relevant to them and that is not public knowledge. Now you are building credibility as a source of value. Next time you invite them to a party, they will come.


Then you can ask if they had a good time, if they met interesting people. Now their “Yes” works for you. Let them think about whether you were interesting and what opportunities they could pursue with you. Surely other people at the party did provide enough social validation by that time.


How to structure the party to not feel like a funnel and be genuinely fun of course is the cherry on top.
