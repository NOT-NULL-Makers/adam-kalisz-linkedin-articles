There was another Prague PostgreSQL Meetup last Monday with P2D2 following that week. Now that dust settled and I came around to write up my notes.


Teresa Lopes talked about large databases at Adyen and how to handle them well. Most of those databases are larger than 64 TB. The one trick one could discard as premature optimization is “getting your columns in order” and avoid  a lot of padding, which can add up to about 10 % savings which at this scale does matter. Also, upgrading to PostgreSQL 18 may improve things if you have many partitions, thanks to fast-path locks by Tomas Vondra.


Josef Machytka proposed we should utilize AI to tune our database to our specific needs, avoid a lot of the art that performance tuning is today and make the endeavor more sustainable long term as the databases evolve.


Luigi Nardi showed us their workload fingerprinting and their tool DBtune, which allows to extract important queries and provide robust and comparable measurements in the presence of noise. This allows us to answer questions such as “What to tune immediately?” or “What is happening?”. We even had a short live demo.


Thank you Gülçin Yıldırım Jelínek and Mayuresh B. for organizing.
