"New post where I argue that LLM coding agents have changed the economics of programming language choice - and the languages designed for human convenience are no longer the optimal capital allocation. 

The learning curve, long the objection to languages like Clojure, has been zeroed out: as Nathan Marz puts it, "developer familiarity stops being the dominant selection criterion." 

What remains is what Rich Hickey called the difference between simple and easy - and Wes McKinney is already hitting the wall, watching agents "chase their own tails and contextually choke on the bloated codebases they have generated" at 100,000 lines of Go. 

Martin Alderson's data shows Clojure is the most token-efficient of nineteen popular languages, expressing the same logic in roughly a fifth fewer tokens than Python and a third fewer than JavaScript - and when context windows degrade non-linearly, that margin compounds. 

I confront the counterarguments - LLMs are measurably worse at Clojure today, static types provide feedback loops Clojure lacks, and the ecosystem is small - but the distinction between "better at generating functions" and "generates better systems" is the one that matters at scale, IMHO."
